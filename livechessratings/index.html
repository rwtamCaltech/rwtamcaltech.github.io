Red name: Avg. Opp. ELO is 150+ points above or below Current ELO Blue name: fewer than 30 classical games in the last 12 months Red+blue mix: both conditions apply Use the "Hide low-activity (blue/purple) players" toggle below to only see players active enough to clear the 30-game threshold.
Avg Moves column: Green: 1+ std. dev. above the average game length across all tracked players (longer, grindier games) Red: 1+ std. dev. below average (shorter games -- quick decisive results, or quick draws)
Win/Draw/Loss Rate columns: Green: 1+ std. dev. above the mean rate of players in the same 100-pt Avg. Opp. ELO bracket (e.g. 2700-2800) Red: 1+ std. dev. below that bracket's mean Compares players facing similarly-rated opposition, not the whole field -- a 2500-bracket player isn't judged against a 2800-bracket player's rate. Same green-above/red-below rule is applied literally to all three columns, so for Loss Rate "green" flags an unusually high loss rate for that bracket, not a good outcome.
Delta / 12m (site) columns: Published directly by 2700chess.com, not computed by us -- Delta is the live classical rating's change vs. the last official FIDE list; 12m (site) is the site's own trailing-12-month game count across all time controls (not classical-only, so it won't match Games (12mo, classical)).
Labels column: Plain-English tags derived entirely from the colorings already on this row -- nothing new is computed, this just names the combinations. A player can carry several at once. Hover a label for the exact rule. Use the "Labels" filter above to isolate players by tendency. Win/Draw/Loss Rate-derived: Hot streak (green Win Rate), Out of Shape (green Loss Rate) or Completely out of shape (red Win Rate + green Loss Rate), Stable Player (green Draw Rate), Very stable player/rarely goes for it (red Win Rate + green Draw Rate), Rarely draws/Goes for it (red Draw Rate), Rarely loses (red Loss Rate). Name-flag-derived: Low volume (blue name), Farming ELO (red name). Avg Moves-derived: Long Slogger (green), Fast Game (red). Opening-derived: Uses rare opening - white/black (one of that side's top-3 career openings is red in the "Most Common Openings" tables below); In book (white/black) (all 3 of that side's top openings are green); Risk Taker (white/black) (2+ of that side's top openings are non-green); Unorthodox (Risk Taker on both sides). <Opening Name> Player, White/Black: the SAME opening name appears 2+ times in that side's top-3 career openings (different ECO codes/variations sharing one name, e.g. two different Nimzo-Indian Defense lines both cracking a player's top 3) -- a stronger single-opening preference signal than any one top-3 entry alone. Data-driven, so the exact label text depends on the opening; use the Labels filter dropdown to see which ones occurred. Workhorse: Games (12mo, classical) is 1+ std. dev. above the mean across all tracked players (whole field, not bracketed). Seen better days: current classical rating is 100+ points below this player's best-ever classical rating (hover the label for the peak value and month/year). Format-comparison labels (classical vs. rapid vs. blitz world rank -- a LOWER rank number is better; "N spots better" means rank_a <= rank_b - N): Elite in all time formats (top 20 world rank in all three); Prefers longer time formats (classical rank 25+ spots better than BOTH rapid and blitz); Prefer rapid (rapid rank 25+ spots better than BOTH classical and blitz); Blitzer (blitz rank 25+ spots better than BOTH classical and rapid, OR -- if not already Elite -- simply top 20 in blitz on its own, OR Blitz ELO is strictly the highest of Current/Rapid/Blitz ELO); Prefer faster time formats (BOTH rapid and blitz rank 25+ spots better than classical, regardless of which of the two is better -- can co-occur with Prefer rapid or Blitzer). Elite in all time formats - Women: same top-20-in-all-three rule, but 2700chess itself silently scopes "world rank" to the women's pool (not the open pool) for female players -- no field on the site discloses this, it was confirmed by inspection (e.g. Hou Yifan shows world rank 1/1/1 in classical/rapid/blitz, true only within the women's pool). Kept as a separate label rather than lumped into the open-pool one so the two aren't conflated. Sex comes from FIDE's own public rating list, not from 2700chess. Good Rapid (Recency) / Good Blitz (Recency): that time control's rating gained more than 10 points from the first to the last point in the trailing-12-month chart (see Rapid Δ / Blitz Δ columns). Out of shape for faster time formats (recency): BOTH Rapid Δ (12mo) and Blitz Δ (12mo) are below -15 points. Rapid workhorse (recency) / Blitz workhorse (recency): Rapid/Blitz Games (12mo) is 1+ std. dev. above the mean across all tracked players; Few rapid (recency) / Few blitz (recency): 1+ std. dev. below the mean (same whole-field convention as Workhorse). Good Classical Growth Past Year / Great Classical Growth Past Year / Regression Classical Growth Past Year: based on the ELO Progression (12mo) column (classical rating, first point to last point in that chart) -- Good fires above +35, Great replaces Good above +50 (mutually exclusive, never both at once), Regression fires below -35.
Rapid Games (12mo) / Blitz Games (12mo) columns: Green: 1+ std. dev. above the mean across all tracked players (backs the Rapid/Blitz workhorse (recency) labels) Red: 1+ std. dev. below the mean (backs the Few rapid/blitz (recency) labels) Hover a colored value for its exact z-score.
Women only toggle: Filters the table to players whose sex is 'F' per FIDE's own public rating list (2700chess.com's player pages don't publish a gender field) -- see the "Elite in all time formats - Women" entry above for why this matters.
